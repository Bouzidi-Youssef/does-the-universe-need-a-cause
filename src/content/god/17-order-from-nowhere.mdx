<div class="prose">
  <p>The universe is not just a brute collection of matter. It obeys precise mathematical laws. Its constants are tuned to produce stable atoms, long-lived stars, and complex chemistry. The ratio between the strengths of electromagnetism and gravity differs by a factor of ten to the thirty-sixth power — and yet both values are exactly what life requires. This is not one coincidence. It is dozens of them, compounded. The life-permitting region of the parameter space, as shown in Chapter I, is a thread running through an enormous landscape of dead configurations.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Nature itself produces order — ice crystals, orbital mechanics, spiral galaxies. Why does the universe's order require anything beyond physics?</p>
  <p>Ice crystals form because the laws of physics impose structure on water molecules under certain conditions. But the question is not why water forms crystals given the laws of physics. The question is why there are laws of physics at all — precise, elegant, mathematically expressible laws, rather than chaos or nothing. The order that crystals exhibit is downstream of the laws. The laws themselves are not explained by anything within the system they govern. Every attempt to explain the laws by more fundamental laws only defers the question: why those more fundamental laws?</p>
  <p>Everything we know that exhibits high, specified complexity — a protein, a microprocessor, a symphony — is the product of intelligence. Not because we define it that way, but because intelligence is the only cause we have ever observed that produces this kind of outcome reliably. Chance produces low-complexity random sequences. Physical laws produce low-complexity regular patterns. Specified complexity — the kind that carries information and serves a function — consistently traces back to a mind. The fine-tuning of the universe exhibits this structure at a scale that dwarfs anything produced by any human intelligence.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">The multiverse handles fine-tuning without a mind — if there are enough universes with varying constants, life-permitting ones will appear by chance.</p>
  <p>The multiverse does not eliminate the need for intelligence. It relocates the specification. For a multiverse to produce universes with randomly varying constants, it requires a mechanism — a process that generates universes, assigns constants, and varies them across some range. That mechanism must itself have a specific structure. Why does the mechanism have the structure that produces life-permitting universes as possible outputs? Why does it produce universes at all, rather than nothing? The intelligence is not explained away. It is pushed one level up — into the design of the universe-generating process itself.</p>
</div>
