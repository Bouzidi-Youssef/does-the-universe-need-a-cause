<div class="prose">
  <p>An intelligence that produced the universe by accident, or by compulsion, or with no understanding of what it was producing, would not be the kind of intelligence the argument requires. The fine-tuning is not just precision — it is precision toward something. The constants are not merely specific numbers. They are the specific numbers that allow matter to form, chemistry to emerge, and life to develop. The universe is not just ordered; it is ordered toward complexity, toward increasingly structured forms of being, toward consciousness.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Evolution shows that purpose-looking outcomes can arise without any purpose. The appearance of direction is an illusion produced by natural selection.</p>
  <p>Natural selection explains how populations change given heritable variation, differential reproduction, and time. It does not explain why the universe has the kind of physics that permits heritable variation. It does not explain why chemistry can encode information in molecular sequences. It does not explain why there are stable atoms for chemistry to work with, or stars to forge those atoms, or planets for them to collect on. Evolution is a mechanism that operates within a framework the argument is asking about. The framework itself — the physical constants, the laws of chemistry, the structure of spacetime — is not produced by evolution. Something set the parameters within which evolution became possible.</p>
  <p>More precisely: the argument from wisdom is not merely that the universe looks designed. It is that the necessary being, which we have already established must exist, must have had a complete understanding of what it was bringing into existence. A being that produces, from no prior material, a precise mathematical structure capable of generating stars, chemistry, and consciousness — without knowing what it is doing — is incoherent. Producing a specific outcome requires knowing what the outcome is. Wisdom, in this sense, is not an additional attribute grafted on from outside. It follows from what the being must have done.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Maybe the necessary being produced the universe by necessity — its nature just is such that the universe follows from it, without deliberation or knowledge.</p>
  <p>This is Spinoza's position — everything follows necessarily from the divine nature, without choice or deliberation. It is coherent, but it creates a different problem: if the universe follows necessarily from the divine nature, then the universe could not have been otherwise. Every feature of the universe — these constants, these laws, this structure — would be logically necessary. But we have already established that the universe is contingent: it could have been different. If the necessary being produces the universe without deliberation, by pure logical emanation, then contingency disappears. And if contingency disappears, the original problem disappears with it — which means a Spinozistic account cannot be the right one. The universe's genuine contingency requires a cause that can select among possibilities — not a cause that can only produce one outcome.</p>
</div>
