<div class="prose">
  <p>Revelation does not arrive at each person individually; it moves through three mechanisms, and none of them is arbitrary. A human messenger carries it, because addressing humans in human language and form is what communication with humans requires; the messenger is held to have delivered the message intact, and is supported by a miracle because a claim needs evidence beyond ordinary capacity. The message is then committed to writing, because it must outlast the messenger's own lifetime, preserved and transmitted en masse — mass transmission across independent lines is what guards a text against quiet alteration. And the message leaves a historical trace in the lives and societies that received it — not sufficient evidence on its own, but corroborating what the other two already establish.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Why not send revelation directly to everyone, bypassing messengers and books entirely?</p>
  <p>Direct certainty for all would eliminate the test; a single transmitted message keeps a single community anchored to one account rather than as many private ones as there are people; and a human messenger is simply what it looks like for an infinite being to speak to a finite one.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Why one final messenger, rather than a new one for every era?</p>
  <p>Sealing the message in one final messenger preserves unity across time the same way a single transmitted message preserves unity across a population; a message held to be complete needs no successor amending it; and reason remains available to apply what was already delivered, without requiring a fresh miracle in every generation.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Why does the messenger need a miracle at all?</p>
  <p>A claim needs evidence; a miracle is that evidence, tied specifically to the one making the claim — not a condition on the message's content, but a condition on trusting its source.</p>
</div>
