<div class="prose">
  <p>Inspiration is internal. It could come from sound reasoning, imagination, intuition, or something less reliable — there is no way, from the inside, to be certain of its source. Revelation is claimed as external: a communication from a specific source, carrying content and authority, requiring both a medium — a messenger — and a message, whether text or transmitted meaning.</p>
  <p>Because revelation issues commands rather than mere information, an order presupposes the authority of whoever gives it. An unproven claim to speak for God is, on its face, indistinguishable from any other unproven claim. So revelation cannot rest on assertion; it has to meet conditions that separate it from a claim that merely resembles it.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Anyone can claim to be a prophet. What actually distinguishes the claim from the real thing?</p>
  <p>Six conditions, taken together, are what does the separating. No single one is sufficient by itself — a message could satisfy several and still fail on one — but a message that fails any of them has already disqualified itself, regardless of how it fares on the rest.</p>
</div>
