<div class="prose">
  <p>All of the above presupposes that the program exists — that there is a substrate, a set of laws, a mathematical structure in which the simulation runs. But the Big Bang model says nothing about where any of that came from. Push the equations of General Relativity backward and you hit a singularity — density goes to infinity, the equations break down, and reliable prediction stops. Physics can describe the universe from a tiny fraction of a second after the beginning. It cannot describe the beginning itself.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Fine — so maybe the universe simply always existed. Eternal, no beginning, no need for a cause.</p>
  <p>This is the second hypothesis — and it has its own deep problem. If the universe always existed in a stable, hot, dense state, something must account for why expansion began at t = 0 rather than never, or a billion years earlier, or later. An eternal system in a stable state does not spontaneously change without a law governing when and how it changes — and that law is already something that must be explained. Eternity going forward from a starting point is coherent. But a past extending infinitely backward that nonetheless terminates in a specific event at a specific moment requires a principle that selects that moment. Such a principle is not free. It is itself a fact demanding explanation.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">What about the multiverse? If infinitely many universes are always being generated, maybe no single cause is needed.</p>
  <p>The multiverse doesn't resolve this — it relocates it. The multiverse itself exists, operates according to laws, and generates universes by some mechanism. All of those things exist and must be accounted for. Every answer of this form defers rather than explains. The ignorance of a cause is not the same as the absence of a cause.</p>
