<div class="prose">
  <p>Science never accepts a brute fact as a final answer. When a measurement doesn't fit the model, researchers don't write "unexplained — brute fact" in the margin and move on. They propose new models, search for hidden variables, assume that something is missing from the picture. The demand for explanation is not switched on selectively. It runs continuously, applied to every phenomenon without exception — until one.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">But there has to be a stopping point somewhere. You can't explain everything — at some level you just have to accept that something is.</p>
  <p>That is true. The question is where the stopping point is, and why. The weak form of the principle stops at the universe itself: the laws of physics, the initial conditions, the fact that there is a universe at all — these are declared to require no explanation. But what is the argument for stopping precisely here? Not: what makes it comfortable to stop here. What makes it principled?</p>
  <p>The stopping point is drawn at the boundary of physics. Physics cannot describe what lies before the singularity, so the habit of explanation stops there too. But the boundary of physics is an epistemic boundary — it tells us where our current equations break down. It says nothing about whether an explanation exists beyond it. We cannot observe the interior of a black hole either. We do not conclude from that inability that black hole interiors are brute facts. We infer, model, reason beyond the reach of direct observation. The limit of the instrument is not the limit of the question.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">Still, maybe the universe is genuinely different — it is the totality of everything, so there is simply nothing outside it to serve as an explanation.</p>
  <p>That is a substantive philosophical claim, and it may be correct. But notice that it is a claim — one that needs to be argued and defended, not assumed. To say "there is nothing outside the universe to serve as an explanation" is not the same as saying "no explanation is required." It is one possible answer to the question of what the explanation is. The weak form does not make this argument. It does not conclude that the universe is inexplicable after careful analysis. It simply stops asking. And a principle of explanation that stops asking at the most important case, without argument, is not a principle. It is a habit with a ceiling.</p>
</div>
