<div class="prose">
  <p>Dark matter has never been directly detected. No instrument has captured a dark matter particle. What physicists observe are effects: galaxies rotating at speeds that the visible mass cannot account for, gravitational lensing stronger than it should be, large-scale cosmic structure that would not have formed without something holding it together. From those effects, scientists infer an unseen cause. They do not say "the galaxy rotates that way for no reason." They say "there must be something we have not yet found." The inference is not tentative speculation — it is treated as one of the firmest conclusions in contemporary cosmology.</p>
  <p class="q-lead">But that is an inference within physics, about a physical entity. Inferring a cause outside the universe is a completely different kind of move.</p>
  <p>The domain is different. The logical structure of the inference is identical. In both cases, observed facts cannot be made coherent without positing an unobserved cause. In both cases, the cause is inferred from its effects rather than detected directly. In both cases, the inference is driven by the demand that the observations be explicable — that there be a reason for what we see. The only thing that differs is where the cause is located. Dark matter is within the universe, so the inference is called physics. A necessary cause is outside the universe, so the inference is called metaphysics. But that distinction is about domain, not method. And the decision to accept inference in one domain while rejecting it in another is not itself a scientific decision. It is a philosophical one.</p>
  <p>Physicists do not say dark matter is one hypothesis among many equally plausible options. They say the observations demand it. The same structure applies here: a contingent universe — one that could have been otherwise, or not existed at all — demands a sufficient reason for its existence. That reason cannot itself be contingent, or the same question simply re-opens one level up. The inference to a non-contingent cause follows by the same logic that makes dark matter necessary: the observations don't make sense without it. The discomfort is not with the method. It is with the destination.</p>
